# Chiambucket — Project Overview

Personal portfolio/personal website for Braven Chiam. Dark-themed, design-forward. The current design language is **"Dark Sensory / Signal Archive"** (July 2026 redesign): every page rides a fixed WebGL shader-gradient field, content sits on dark "instrument panels", and one warm ember accent is reserved for live/status moments. Built with Next.js and deployed on Vercel.

**September 2026 clarity + performance pass (branch `tweak/dark-simplify`).** Same design, lighter and plainer. Keep these rules:
- **Background (October 2026, branch `task/background-cards`):** the WebGL shader is gone. `components/Backdrop.tsx` renders one fixed, static `.sa-backdrop` layer (CSS in the "BACKDROP" section at the end of `mainstyle.css`): a soft pool of light at the top of the screen over the warm base (`--sa-base`, cool on `/contact` + `/homelab`), with a faint perfboard dot grid masked to that light. No canvas, no JS, no animation; colours follow `--sa-accent`. The film-grain overlay, cursor spotlight and section glow orbs are retired. Nothing may animate continuously while a page is idle: timers (the tools carousel, the blink easter egg) run only while on screen, and `LazyVideo` pauses loops scrolled off screen.
- **Project cards:** flagship and highlight projects (`data-flagship` / `data-highlight`) keep full cards with their gold edge, two to a row on the homepage. Ordinary projects use compact horizontal cards (`.hp-pf-card.is-compact`). Projects with no write-up (`.is-soon`: Kauli, Series One Light, Copy Board, Web Development, Minecraft) are muted grey and show a non-link "Coming soon" label; their peek summaries say "Write-up coming soon" and keep any live demo link. Nothing links to `/comingsoon` from the project cards or summaries. "Read article" is a real `<a href>`.
- **`backdrop-filter` is opt-in glass only.** A global rule at the end of `mainstyle.css` still forces it off everywhere, so the dark design's old blur rules stay dead (blur over the fixed background cost a re-render per scroll frame). The Apple-style glass surfaces (the sunlit homepage and the nav pills, October 2026) opt back in with `!important`. Keep blur off surfaces nested inside other glass: a nested blur only sees its parent's tint and turns grey.
- **No AI sparkle icons anywhere.** The old uiverse "sparkle" hero button (`.button-hero`, `.dots_border`) and the ✦ glyph after "Read the summary" were removed; "View my work" is a plain `.hp-btn`.
- No looping decorative animations (orbs, aura drift, headline glow, badge pulse, scroll hint, word rotators). **Exception: the sunlit homepage is a "living poster"** (see below), but its loops are transform/opacity only and run only while the hero or closing section is on screen.
- Labels are plain: `--font-ddt` now points at DM Sans, no wide all-caps tracking, no index numbers in the nav, chapter rail or section headers (the big `01/02/03` `.seh-number`/`.peh-number` numerals were removed in October 2026), no `FIG ·` prefix, no bracketed kickers, no `.sa-hud` corner readouts, no Flagship/Highlight tier badges.
- Copy is direct and factual: no mannered prose, no em dashes in visible text, titles `"<Name> | Braven Chiam"`.
- Article loops use `LazyVideo` with posters in `public/images/posters/`.

## Languages (i18n)

The site is in English plus Simplified Chinese (`zh`), Japanese (`ja`), Dutch (`nl`), German (`de`), Russian (`ru`), French (`fr`) and Spanish (`es`). Only these pages are translated (`LOCALIZED` in `lib/i18n.ts`): home, contact, photography, homelab, archive, credits, AI use (and the 404). **The articles stay English** (their links never get a prefix).

- **URLs:** English lives at the plain paths; each other language has the same page under its code (`/ja`, `/ja/contact`). The pages are `app/[lang]/<route>/page.tsx` (prerendered per language by `app/[lang]/layout.tsx`, `dynamicParams = false`) rendering the same page component as the English route.
- **Language choice:** `proxy.ts` sends a request for a localized English page to the visitor's language: the `lang` cookie (set by the nav globe) or else `Accept-Language` (`pickLang`; Traditional Chinese has no match, so stays English). Choosing English with the globe sets `lang=en`, which stops the redirect.
- **Helpers (`lib/i18n.ts`):** `splitLang(pathname)` gives `{ lang, base }` (base = the English path; use it for any `pathname === '/'`-style check), `localize(href, lang)` prefixes links to localized pages (articles untouched), `langAlternates(base)` / `lib/langMeta.ts` build hreflang + canonical + OG locale metadata. Client components read the language with `useLang()` (`lib/useLang.ts`). `<html lang>` is set before paint by the inline head script in `app/layout.tsx` and on navigation by `ClientEffects`.
- **Copy:** the shell words (nav, footer, globe, pop-up close, AI-use mark label) are `lib/shell.ts`. Each page's words live beside it: small pages in `<area>/_copy.tsx` (a record of languages; translations in `_copy.<lang>.tsx`), large ones in `<area>/_copy/<lang>.tsx` with an `index.ts` loader map so only the visitor's language is loaded (the server page loads it and passes it down). `en.tsx` defines the type (`typeof en`), so a missing or misshapen translation is a type error. Peeks: `components/peeks/<lang>.tsx` (loaded with `next/dynamic`, preloaded by the homepage). **When you change English copy, change every language** (or hand it to a translator with `.plan/i18n-translation-guide.md`, which holds the voice, register per language, glossary and the rule that UI labels must not get longer).
- **Language switch (`components/LangGlobe.tsx`, styles in `components/nav.css` `.pnav-lang*`, `.pnav-sheet-lang`):** on desktop a globe is the last item on the right of the nav, outside the merging pieces, with its own glass, fading away past 48px of scroll. On phones (≤760px) the globe is hidden and takes no room (so the merged bar stays at the edge); the language is the last row of the menu dropdown (`LangSheet`), which opens the list in place. On English-only pages it shows a note in the reader's language (cookie or browser) that the article is English for now.
- **Type (`app/i18n.css`):** zh/ja drop the tight Latin tracking on headings and labels; de/nl/ru may hyphenate. The display faces are Latin-only, so CJK and Cyrillic fall back to system faces per glyph.
- **Every change ships in every language (hard rule).** A new page is not done until it has its `_copy` in all 8 languages, an `app/[lang]/<route>/page.tsx`, its path in `LOCALIZED` (`lib/i18n.ts`) and `config.matcher` (`proxy.ts`), and its 7 language alternates in `public/sitemap.xml`. Any edit to English copy (`en.tsx`, `_copy.tsx`, `peeks/en.tsx`, `recs/en.ts`, `lib/shell.ts`, `lib/articleShell.ts`) changes the same strings in every other language **in the same commit**, written per `.plan/i18n-translation-guide.md` (Braven's cheerful, conversational voice; labels no longer than the English). New visible text goes into the copy, never straight into JSX. `scripts/check-i18n.mjs` enforces this: the pre-commit hook (`.githooks/pre-commit`, wired by `npm install` via `prepare`) blocks a commit that changes an English copy file without its translations, `prebuild` fails the build when a page or translation is missing, and `npm run check:i18n` also flags translations older than their English. Only a commit that truly leaves visible English unchanged may skip the hook (`I18N_SKIP=1 git commit`). The few deliberate English-only exceptions are listed in the script (`ENGLISH_ONLY`).
- **Checking a change:** look at the page in English and at least one long language (de or ru) and one CJK language (ja or zh) at 1440px and 390px; nothing may overflow or wrap inside a button or pill.

## Shared components (`components/`)

- **`ArticleRecommendations.tsx`** (`'use client'`) — renders a "More to explore" project card grid at the end of every article page. Self-contained: includes all project data, the peek-modal system (same chapter content as homepage), and a "Load more" button (shows 3 cards initially, all on click). Takes an `exclude` prop (the current article's project ID, e.g. `"proj-june"`). Import it at the bottom of any article `main` element.
- **`ProjectPeek.tsx`** (`'use client'`) — the shared "peek" reader (`PeekFeed`) used by BOTH the homepage gallery and `ArticleRecommendations`. Opening a peek shows the clicked project's reader; under it the next project's cover peeks up, blurred (`PullNext`, `.hp-rd-pull*`). Each project in the feed is its own card (`.hp-rd-feed-item`, a whiter sheet on a faintly tinted tray, `.hp-modal-content` padding `--pad`) with a labelled gap (`.hp-rd-feed-sep`, "Next: …") before the next one, so the projects read as separate write-ups. Scrolling or swiping past the end pulls it up with resistance (`--pull` 0 to 1, `PULL_POP` px); let go early and it springs back, pull far enough (about three wheel notches) or click it and it pops open with a spring. The gesture that arrives at the end (a wheel run whose events are under `GESTURE_GAP` 220ms apart, momentum included, or a drag that started above the end) can only lift it to `ARRIVAL_LIFT` (0.28) and never opens it, so a fast scroll is stopped at the end first; opening takes a fresh scroll or swipe that starts at the end. Trackpad momentum keeps sending wheel events after the fingers lift, so a new swipe inside that stream is recognised by its speed climbing again after at least three slowing events (it too only pulls if it starts at the end); a part-pulled card springs back `PULL_IDLE` (360ms) after the last event, so slow single wheel clicks still add up. Once open it joins the feed and scrolls into view, and the one after it peeks up in turn, until the END OF FEED marker. The reader's close button is a solid chip so it never sits on text. Every project id needs a `PEEK_ORDER` entry, a `COVERS` entry in `components/peeks/kit.tsx`, and `TITLES` plus a `PeekBody` case in EVERY language file `components/peeks/<lang>.tsx` (English is `en.tsx`; the reader loads only the visitor's language, see Languages). It is keyed by `startId` at the call sites, so the expand state resets on each open.
- **Embeds (`lib/embed.ts`)** — any route ending in `/embed` is meant for another site's iframe: `Nav`, `Footer` and `SceneShell` render nothing there. `/stackchan-lidar/embed` is the article's "Try the scan" playground alone, filling the frame (noindex, canonical to the article): `<iframe src="https://www.chiambucket.com/stackchan-lidar/embed" allow="fullscreen">`. Nothing blocks framing (no `X-Frame-Options`/`frame-ancestors` in `vercel.json`).
- **`ArticleLinks.tsx`** (server component) — a row of key-link pills (`ArtLink[]` with `type: 'github' | 'video' | 'demo' | 'cad' | 'download'`, each with `label` + `url`) surfaced in the article hero, just after `.art-toolrow`. ## Per-page accent theming

Pages are recoloured by a single `data-theme` attribute on `<html>` so each route reads distinctly while staying cohesive. All hues are sampled from the **Frame 6 gradient** (navy → lavender → mauve → violet), saved in `temporary screenshots/Frame 6.png`.

- **`lib/theme.ts`** — `THEME_MAP` (route → scene theme), `themeForPath()`, `applyTheme()`. Superseded in October 2026 by the painted scenes (see the scenes note at the top): the default is now `night` (articles, 404), and the day routes map to `sunlit`, `golden`, `noon`, `teal` and `mist`. The older palettes (`paper`, `indigo`, `datacenter`, `blue`/`violet`/`mauve`/`steel`) still exist as `html[data-theme]` blocks in `mainstyle.css` but no route uses them. Green stays reserved for presence, ember for status.
- **No hardcoded accent colours.** Accent glows/borders/links use `rgba(var(--sa-accent), x)` (per-theme) or the `--hp-*` / `--em-grad` / `--title-grad` tokens, never a literal blue. The July 2026 warm-default pass converted the remaining hardcoded blues (`94,121,219` indigo, `127,168,255` / `140,170,255` sky, `0,107,179`/`10,126,199`/`10,95,176` blue, plus `#5e79db`/`#7fa8ff` colour uses) to those tokens so they follow the route theme.
- **Desktop scale.** The layout is rem/clamp based; `html` root font-size steps down on large screens (`93.75%` ≥1100px, `90.6%` ≥1600px) so the site doesn't read zoomed-in at 1080p. Mobile keeps the full 16px base.

Auras/buttons/links/gradient text follow automatically — don't hardcode accent rgba values, use the tokens.
- **The scenes follow the same themes:** each theme name has a preset in `PRESETS` (`components/SunlitScene.tsx`) and a sky block in `app/scenes.css`; night pages also take their `--sa-*`/`--hp-*` tokens from the night block there.

## CSS conventions

- Single file `public/mainstyle.css`; sections delineated by comments.
- Fonts via local `@font-face`: `oswaldreg`, `oswaldbold`, `inter`, `dmsans`, `gcreg`, `ddt`, `GreaterTheory`, `mextrine`, `monda`, `roboto` (in `public/fonts/`).
- Pair a display face (oswald/gcreg) with a body face (dmsans/inter). Tight tracking on big headings, generous line-height on body.
- Colors: pure black `#000`, off-white text `#e8e8e8`/`#d8d8d8`, accent blue `#0a7ec7`/`#006bb3`, indigo `#5e79db`, sky `#7fa8ff`. Glassmorphism via `var(--hp-glass)` + `backdrop-filter`.

- **iPhone Safari's bars (iOS 26).** Pages use `viewport-fit=cover` with no theme colour, so Safari shows the page through its status bar and toolbar. Safari instead paints a solid colour when a tap at the screen's top or bottom edge would land inside a `position: fixed`/`sticky` full-width layer. So pinned decorative layers (`.sl-scene`, `.sl-grass`, `.pnav-blob`, `.ls-bg`) and the pinned stages (`.hs-stage`, `.hs-reader-stage`, `.hs-fl-stage`, `.hs-bench-pin`, `.ls-sticky`, `.pp-sticky`) are `visibility: hidden` with visible children, and the full-bleed pictures/canvases inside the stages (`.hs-art`, `.hs-under`, `.hs-reader-scene`, `.hs-fl-scene`) are `pointer-events: none`. Keep new pinned layers the same way, and check with `document.elementFromPoint(innerWidth / 2, 3)`: no fixed or sticky ancestor. Fixed bars narrower than 80% of the width are ignored.
- **Videos that only feed WebGL** (Pip's tablet screen) must be real, opaque elements on the page (160 × 90, laid under an opaque layer). Safari refuses to autoplay a muted video it counts as hidden. Chrome on iOS plays them anyway, so test in Safari.

## Copy / content conventions

- **Write all copy in the `connected-prose` style** (the skill of that name): plain, full sentences joined with connectors, plain labels without full stops, and only what a visitor cares about, with no production notes. `app/pip/page.tsx` is the model.
- **No em dashes (`—`).** Use commas or split into sentences. Applies to all user-facing copy.
- **No implementation details in reader-facing copy** (Braven, 2026-11-20: "the reader does not care about implementation details"). Say what a thing does for the reader, not how it was built or why a download behaves a certain way: no notes on how a 3D model was assembled, why a firmware image lacks a password, sensor angles, scan step sizes and the like, unless that detail is the point of the section.
- **AI-use mark first.** Wherever the `AiMark` sits beside category tags (article heroes, feature cards, gallery and recommendation cards, the peek hero), it comes first, then the category pill(s).
- When adding a page: update `public/sitemap.xml` + `public/llms.txt`, add the right JSON-LD, and ensure `metadata` is on a server component.
